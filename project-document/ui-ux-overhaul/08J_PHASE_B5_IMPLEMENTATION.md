## Investigation before implementation

The affected traceability entries are F18 (WebGL board), F19 (fallback), F20 (camera), and the presentation paths for dice, movement, construction and money (`project-document/monopoly-websockets/FEATURE_TRACEABILITY.md`). Canonical changes belong in `Client/game-board.instruction.md` and the client motion/accessibility checklist. No server, shared contract, protocol or persistence change is planned.

The B4 real-session evidence in `08I_PHASE_B4_IMPLEMENTATION.md` records 16.3–31.5 actual scene FPS and 21.2–25.0 ms median GPU time across random multiplayer rolls on Intel UHD. Those rolls are evidence of a rendering capacity problem, not a controlled B5 baseline. B4 already fixed the scheduling ceiling and the on-screen actual-scene counter; its idle, resource and shader-warm-up checks passed. B1–B3 already addressed transient resources, React fan-out, first-use shader links and lobby scene startup. B5 will retain those boundaries.

The historical Phase A audit (`08A_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT.md`, PERF-04/07/08/09/10) found substantial costs in native pixel ratio, image-based lighting, the PBR tabletop and High post-processing. Those are hypotheses to remeasure on this B4 head. Shadow-map rendering was relatively cheap in that audit (PERF-23), so removing shadows is not a default choice. `renderQuality.ts`, `GameScene.tsx`, `Tabletop.tsx`, `StudioEnvironment.tsx` and `ScenePostEffects.tsx` confirm that the current tiers still share a 1.25 render ratio on the available 125% display, all materials receive the studio environment, Low/Balanced use a PBR table, and High uses a four-sample post chain. The scene remains demand-rendered, with baked studio PMREM, one key shadow light and a post chain only at High.

The original Iris Xe / Apple M1 class reference device is unavailable. The local comparison machine is an Intel UHD Graphics 0x9B41 using ANGLE D3D11 in headed Chrome 155, about 60 Hz, at 1522×770 CSS pixels and display DPR 1.25. GPU timer queries are supported. Benchmarks use deterministic UAT fixtures at a 120 FPS cap and the same browser/window/diagnostics settings for both builds. Real multiplayer rolls will be reported separately as observational because dice and concurrent tab load differ between runs.

Candidate work will be evaluated one meaningful step at a time: pixel ratio policy for Low/Balanced; cheaper tabletop shading while preserving the oak texture and contact cues; selective image-based lighting for glossy objects; and High post-chain multisampling. Each candidate needs a like-for-like GPU/FPS result and matched visual captures. Rejected candidates and visible differences will be recorded here.

## Engineering choices and controlled experiments

The investigation rejected removing key-light shadows: the historical A/B attributed only about 2 ms to all Balanced shadows, and they communicate building height and ownership. It also rejected replacing the wood tabletop with a flat backdrop: the same oak texture, under-board decal and tier-dependent lighting remain. B6 adaptive quality, frame scheduling, gameplay timing and the B3 persistent scene were left untouched.

1. **Pixel budget and table.** Low now clamps the scene to DPR 1 and shades the oak texture with an unlit material. Balanced uses Lambert shading that still receives the key shadow. High retains the full PBR table. The first candidate used Balanced DPR 1.1 on the 125% display. In the deterministic warm walk, Low changed from 40.0 FPS / 19.7 ms median GPU to 60.0 FPS / 9.2 ms. Balanced changed from 42.1 FPS / 20.4 ms to 48.1 FPS / 17.8 ms. This candidate left the environment lookup in place, isolating the remaining major cost.
2. **Selective environment.** Balanced removes the scene-wide PMREM lookup from matte materials; coins and kit glass still receive that same PMREM directly. Its hemisphere fill doubles to recover diffuse brightness. Low and High keep their scene environment. At the unchanged Balanced DPR 1.1, the fixed walk changed from 17.8 to 10.3 ms median GPU (48.1 to 59.0 FPS) before fill tuning. The initial unfilled capture was too dark, so it was not kept. The fill-adjusted walk measured 9.5 ms / 57.2 FPS in a separate run.
3. **Balanced sharpness.** The 896×414 capture showed that DPR 1.1 softened very small tile names. Raising the Balanced range to 1.1–1.2 gave 12.0 ms median GPU and 58.8 FPS in the same warm walk on the 125% display, still below the historical 13 ms GPU goal. Dice measured 12.3 ms / 56.1 FPS and construction 10.3 ms / 60.1 FPS. This is the selected quality policy. The small-viewport before/after for this single change differs by 0.91 mean channel value; the extra samples make letters and diagonals a little firmer without restoring the original GPU cost.
4. **High post chain.** Four composer MSAA samples cost substantially on the available iGPU. Two samples retain multisampling, N8AO, bloom, vignette, the studio environment and the premium PBR table. The fixed High walk changed from 61.6 to 53.6 ms median GPU in the first isolated experiment and 56.3 ms in a later full replay; the corresponding FPS was 17.9, 19.9 and 19.1. This is a modest improvement; High remains unsuitable for 60 FPS on this iGPU. The matched High board capture differs by 0.48 mean channel value at 1280×720 DPR 1, chiefly at edges, and was visually negligible in engineering review. A zero-sample composer could save more but had greater edge-quality risk, so it was not selected.

The implementation is limited to `apps/client/src/game/scene/render/` and one tier-switch expectation, plus capture tooling's explicit `--dpr` option. No wire contract, product version, room snapshot, server authority or gameplay rules changed; no protocol/snapshot bump is needed. The same wood texture and baked studio asset are retained, so no new texture download is introduced. The final runtime bundle and resource checks are recorded below.

## Measurement protocol and comparability

The unchanged B4 bundle and each B5 candidate were built from their exact worktree state with `pnpm perf:benchmark --build`, which uses production React and an unminified UAT bundle. All frame measurements used headed Chrome 155 on the same Intel UHD 0x9B41 / ANGLE D3D11 adapter, a 1522×770 CSS viewport, device DPR 1.25, ~59.9 Hz refresh, 120 FPS setting, diagnostics on, two warm deterministic replays of the same fixture and working GPU timer queries. The final suite covers idle, dice, walk, purchase, rent and two-house construction at Low, Balanced and High. It measures actual WebGL scene submissions and timer-query GPU elapsed time; intervals are contiguous active-animation intervals, not idle gaps. CPU callback cost, draw calls, triangle requests and renderer resource counts are also present in the raw reports.

The benchmark's generic `perf:compare` tool correctly warns that the *internal drawing buffer* changed: this is an intentional effect of the B5 render-ratio policy. The CSS viewport, physical display DPR, hardware, fixture and cap were held constant, so the before/after is a product-level comparison at the same player display conditions. The Balanced selective-environment experiment was also measured with exactly the same internal buffer (1646×819) before and after, isolating the environment change from render scale. No claim of a like-for-like random multiplayer roll is made.

## Final performance results

The selected-policy full suite used the same deterministic conditions stated above. Warm walk values are the most detailed summary; the [baseline](./08J_evidence/baseline.json) and [selected-policy](./08J_evidence/final.json) reports contain each replay and every metric.

| Tier | Actual walk FPS | Median GPU | GPU P95 | Active interval P50 / P95 / P99 | CPU callback P95 | Draw calls / triangles (median) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low before → B5 | 40.0 → 60.0 | 19.7 → 9.4 ms | 29.6 → 15.1 ms | 16.8 / 34.9 / 50.1 → 16.6 / 16.8 / 16.9 ms | 6.2 → 6.7 ms | 141 / 79,710 → same |
| Balanced before → B5 | 42.1 → 59.8 | 20.4 → 10.6 ms | 27.5 → 14.7 ms | 16.7 / 33.5 / 49.9 → 16.7 / 16.8 / 16.9 ms | 4.9 → 8.5 ms | 159 / 110,562 → same |
| High before → B5 | 17.9 → 18.5 | 61.6 → 57.2 ms | 66.2 → 69.0 ms | 66.5 / 99.8 / 116.4 → 50.2 / 99.9 / 116.5 ms | 10.4 → 8.4 ms | 180 / 121,189 → same |

The controlled dice, purchase, rent and build fixtures also reached 59.6–60.1 FPS at Low/Balanced; their median GPU times were 9.2–10.1 ms (Low) and 10.4–11.5 ms (Balanced), down from 17.5–18.0 and 20.2–23.6 ms respectively. Low/ Balanced active P95 intervals were 16.8 ms in all five cases. Every tier's three-second idle fixture submitted zero scene frames and zero scheduler callbacks. Warm replay program links remained zero. Renderer geometry, texture and program counts were unchanged for each matched scenario (walk: Low 100/49/22, Balanced 103/52/26, High 105/76/42); draw calls and triangles were unchanged. The pixel buffers are smaller by design at Low (DPR 1.25→1) and Balanced (1.25→1.2), so GPU render-target memory should fall, but process GPU memory was not sampled in this phase. The [30-replay soak](./08J_evidence/soak.json) showed zero growth in geometry, texture and program counts at Low and Balanced; JS heap rose 1.87 and 2.03 MB after only three warm-ups, consistent with the existing B2 JIT-sensitive heap caveat. This is not a process GPU-memory measurement.

The historical reference targets are met on this **different, lower-class Intel UHD machine** for the selected fixed walk (Low 9.4 ≤10 ms, Balanced 10.6 ≤13 ms). They are not a guarantee for every scene: Low dice measured 10.1 ms, and Balanced rent 11.5 ms. High GPU cost falls in most fixtures (dice 68.4→55.7 ms, build 65.0→52.8 ms), but the High walk remains around 18–19 FPS with a P99 interval above 100 ms. The High purchase CPU P95 rose from 13.8 to 19.7 ms in the two-replay sample; the cause is unproven and needs wider hardware coverage. Balanced's CPU callback P95 also increased despite lower GPU time and unchanged draw calls; it stayed below a 60 Hz frame budget in these fixtures. Those observations are reported as limits, not CPU gains.
